The rules are written in code, not in a prompt.

Your agent can be wrong, rushed or talked into things. Remit doesn’t depend on it behaving: every purchase passes the same checks before any money moves, and PayPal itself enforces the hold.

Six rules. Every call. No exceptions for a persuasive prompt.

House rules

Your agent decides what to buy. The wallet decides whether it can.

Only you approve money
A plan or top-up opens only after you approve it on PayPal.
Lines stay separate
A purchase can use its own line, then the shared buffer. Never another line.
No overspending
Remit checks first, and PayPal refuses any capture larger than the hold.MAX_CAPTURE_AMOUNT_EXCEEDED
A second opinion
A separate model checks each purchase against its line. If it can’t be reached, Remit refuses.
Refusals stay on record
Every refused purchase is in your ledger with its reason.
One purchase at a time
Two purchases can never slip through the same check at once.

Plug in the agent you already use.

Remit is an MCP server. Add one config block and a token to any MCP-compatible agent, and it can see its lines, propose a plan and pay, one purchase at a time.

The agent never holds PayPal keys. Every call goes through your wallet and its rules.
