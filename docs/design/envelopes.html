Every purchase passes four checks before any money moves.

Claude asks to spend from one envelope, with a price and its reasoning. Remit runs the checks in order and stops at the first one that fails. Pick a purchase.

  1. 1The envelope is in your plan

  2. 2It fits what’s left

  3. 3Purpose check

  4. 4Capture from your PayPal hold

Your money stays in your PayPal account until it’s spent.

Approving a plan places an authorization hold, not a transfer. Each purchase captures only its own price. When the week closes, the rest is released to you.

PayPal won’t void a hold after a partial capture (PREVIOUSLY_CAPTURED), so Remit lets the leftover lapse when the authorization expires and says so in your ledger.

House rules, written in code.

Claude decides what to buy. The wallet decides whether it can, and no prompt can change these.

Only you approve money

A budget or top-up opens only after you approve it on PayPal. Claude can ask; it can’t say yes.

Envelopes stay separate

A purchase can use its own envelope, then the shared buffer. Never another envelope.

No overspending

Remit checks first, and PayPal refuses any capture larger than the hold.

MAX_CAPTURE_AMOUNT_EXCEEDED

A second opinion on every purchase

A separate Claude call sees the envelope and the purchase, not the page Claude was shopping on. If it can’t be reached, Remit refuses.

Refusals stay on the record

Every refused purchase is in your ledger with its reason.

One purchase at a time

Spends run one after another, so two can’t slip through the same check at once.

Give any agent a wallet.

Remit’s MCP server works with Claude Desktop, Cursor or your own agent. It holds no PayPal keys. Every call goes to your wallet, which applies the same rules.
