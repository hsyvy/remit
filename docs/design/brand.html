The idea the design is built on

Every dollar has a reason. Every reason has a limit.

Remit is a wallet an AI agent can spend from only after you approve its plan on PayPal. The owner's job is to check, not to chat, so the interface reads like a bank statement that explains itself: amounts in a ledger face, each with its reason beside it, and color used only to say where the money is.

Four product-led landing pages, in the register of the best fintech and consumer-tech sites. I recommend Ledger.

Round three leaned on metaphors (vaults, irises, carved stone), which made it look art-directed rather than professional. The sites people trust with money lead with the product itself, shown perfectly, and let restraint do the rest. So all four of these share one layout idea: the real Remit budget card and activity feed as the hero, with the real numbers from the example week. They differ only in light, type and tone. Pick one and I’ll design the rest of the page and the web app screens in it.

Ledger

Recommended

Warm and editorial, like the best modern banks for founders. A text serif headline, warm off-white, and the product floating on a photograph of morning light on plaster.

Why it works
  • The most trustworthy and the most distinctive of the four: it feels like a bank you’d choose, not a dev tool.
  • The serif carries authority; the product card stays crisp sans with tabular figures.
  • The line under the hero states the PayPal promise plainly, which is what a cautious owner needs first.
Watch out for
  • The light-on-plaster photo has to be a real photograph we own, shot or licensed.
  • Warm off-white with a serif is popular right now; it stays premium only with exact spacing and very good type.

Rails

Best for developers

Precise and technical, like payments infrastructure. Visible column guides, deep navy and one green, and a code panel showing the agent’s purchase call next to its result.

Why it works
  • Shows both audiences at once: the owner sees the budget, the developer sees how the agent spends it.
  • The densest and most informative first screen. The three facts underneath answer the obvious questions.
Watch out for
  • Closest to existing payments sites; less personal for an owner trusting an agent with household money.
  • The code panel and fact row can move into Ledger’s developer section if you pick Ledger.

Clarity

Most minimal

Bright and object-like, like a consumer-hardware product page. Centered headline, black pill button, and the budget card sitting on a pale grey stage as if it were a device.

Why it works
  • The simplest to read: one sentence, one object, one action.
  • The thin $55.20 figure makes the money the hero.
Watch out for
  • Least ownable: with the logo removed, it could be many products.
  • Needs motion (the card filling in as purchases land) to feel alive.

Night

Most premium

The same layout in the dark, lit from above like a product on a studio set. Off-white type, hairline borders, and a single muted mint for money still available.

Why it works
  • Feels like a premium card or pro product. The soft overhead light gives depth without glow.
  • Pairs naturally with a dark web app for people who check it at night.
Watch out for
  • Dark sites can feel less open about money; long explanatory sections are harder to read.
  • Needs an equally careful light mode for the app and statements.
Earlier round: six metaphor concepts (Vault, Departure board, Escapement, Aperture, Envelopes, Inscription)

Live versions: Remit Live Concepts.

Vault

Recommended

Each line of the budget is its own compartment. The hero is a wall of bronze safe-deposit boxes engraved with the plan lines, with the dog food drawer pulled open because that money was just spent.

Why it works
  • Security, privacy and permanence in one picture, and it explains the product: separate compartments that can’t borrow from each other.
  • Bronze, parchment and verdigris green is a palette no other fintech or AI product uses.
  • On the live site the open drawer can follow the ledger: when the agent buys, that line’s box slides out.
Watch out for
  • The hero needs a real high-resolution render made for it; this one is a concept.
  • The bronze plate in the product section is decoration. In the web app the cards should be flat parchment with bronze hairlines.

Departure board

Most memorable

Every dollar has a reason, shown in public. The hero is a split-flap board that flips through the ledger, refusals and pending requests included.

Why it works
  • The most mesmerizing in motion: flaps flipping as each purchase lands is the demo moment.
  • Shows that refusals are part of the product rather than errors to hide.
  • Tabular figures and condensed type make every number line up perfectly.
Watch out for
  • Industrial rather than premium: all-caps condensed type everywhere feels loud for a product that asks for trust.
  • Keep the board to the hero and the ledger; the rest of the site needs a calmer sentence-case voice.

Escapement

Most luxurious

A watch’s escapement releases energy one tick at a time. Remit releases money one capture at a time. Midnight dial blue, rhodium silver, and a ruby used only for the one action.

Why it works
  • The richest image of the three, and the ruby-as-action idea is very ownable.
  • Precision and craft read immediately, with no explanation needed.
Watch out for
  • The metaphor needs a sentence before people get it, and the imagery borrows from luxury watch advertising.
  • The logo came out as a compass needle, and the dial’s four segment colors don’t mean anything yet. Both need redesigning.

Aperture

New

You set the aperture; the agent works inside it. Like a camera iris, the opening is exactly as wide as what you approved and narrows as money is spent.

Why it works
  • The calmest and most premium of the new three: one sculptural object on pale ground.
  • The iris is a live gauge: in the prototype the opening closes by exactly the share each purchase spends.
  • Works as a mark at every size.
Watch out for
  • Camera irises are a familiar motif; the money meaning has to be shown, which the live version does.

Envelopes

New

The cash-envelope method, the oldest household budgeting system, rebuilt for agents. Each line is its own paper envelope.

Why it works
  • Instantly understood by anyone who has budgeted at home. No metaphor to explain.
  • Warm and human without being childish; the four envelope colors become the colors of the four lines everywhere.
Watch out for
  • Less "private bank" than the others. It earns trust through familiarity rather than authority.
  • Pastel tones need care so the money states (spent, held, refused) still read clearly.

Inscription

New

Some rules are set in stone. Remit’s money rules live in server code no prompt can change, so the site carves them in travertine.

Why it works
  • The strongest statement of the core promise: only you approve money, and a prompt can’t change that.
  • Monumental and calm. Lapis blue on stone is unusual and dignified.
Watch out for
  • All-caps inscriptional type is slow to read; keep it for headlines and the rules.
  • Risks feeling heavy or institutional for a hackathon demo.
Earlier round: Midnight, Precision, Atelier (brand photos and app screens)

Midnight

Recommended

Private banking. Deep midnight navy, bone white, and champagne used only for foil and the one thing that needs you. A serif R in a thin square frame, with its leg stopping exactly on the frame's edge: within its remit.

Why it earns trust
  • The most trustworthy of the three. It looks like an institution that has held money for a long time.
  • The monogram carries the idea on its own, and it works blind-embossed, foil-stamped, and at icon size.
  • The dark screen makes the hero balance and the champagne bar the only bright things, so attention goes to the money.
Watch out for
  • The monogram has to be drawn by hand as a vector. In the renders the R's leg doesn't always land exactly on the frame.
  • A dark-first app needs an equally careful light version for daytime and print.
  • Champagne is for one action at a time. Used on every link, it starts to look like a hotel.

Precision

Strong alternative

Swiss and engineered. White, graphite and platinum hairlines, one neo-grotesk typeface, and cobalt only on the approval button. The mark is the spending bar itself: two-thirds solid, one-third outline.

Why it earns trust
  • The clearest screen of the three, and the easiest to build well in the existing app.
  • The bar mark explains the product without words, and it etches cleanly on metal.
  • Reads as modern financial infrastructure, the same league as the best payments companies.
Watch out for
  • Restrained white fintech is common. Its distinctiveness rests entirely on the bar mark and very good typesetting.
  • Lowercase “remit” is friendlier but less formal than the other two.

Atelier

Alternative

Heritage. Forest green, ivory and brass foil, a letterspaced classical serif, and an engraved seal whose line ends in a stop: a limit that is never crossed.

Why it earns trust
  • The most crafted on paper. The foil seal and deckle-edged card feel like a family office.
  • Green ties to money without looking like a bank logo.
  • The seal works as a wax-seal-style mark on approvals and statements.
Watch out for
  • Ivory with a serif is close to a look that many AI-made sites use now. It needs very careful execution to avoid looking generic.
  • The seal's line-and-stop reads a little like a T at small sizes. Draw it as a vector and test it at 16 pixels.
  • It's the hardest to translate into a fast, dense app screen.

Principles

Five rules every screen follows

No amount without a reason

Every figure the agent spent or asked for shows the plan line it came from and why, in the agent's own words. A bare number in the UI is a bug.

Color says where the money is

Solid ink is spent, hatched amber is held, a green outline is coming back, brick red is refused. Blue appears only on things you can press.

Decisions about money come first

A pending budget or top-up sits above everything else on the page. Chat is useful, but it's secondary to the money.

The agent is a named tool

It's called Claude, it has a letter monogram, and it never gets a face, a sparkle or the word "magic". It asks, explains and reports.

Say exactly what PayPal does

Use hold, capture and release, with dates. When PayPal releases money at expiry rather than right away, the screen says so.

Mark and wordmark

A bracket around what was spent

The mark is the product drawn small. The brackets are the limit you approved, the solid block is what the agent has spent, and the empty space is what comes back. It's built on a 32-unit grid with 3-unit strokes, so it stays sharp at 16 pixels.

Don't fill the open space, add a gradient or a glow, put the mark in a rounded app tile with a shadow, or animate the block filling up as decoration. The block's size is fixed; it's a symbol, not a live meter.

Color

Four money states, one action color

Each state also has its own pattern (solid, hatched, outline, struck through), so the bar and the ledger still read in grayscale, when printed, or for people who can't tell amber from green. The swatches switch with your light or dark setting.

Approval

The button names the amount and where it happens. The note under it says what a hold means. Remit never approves money itself.

Nothing is taken until Claude buys something. Whatever is left returns to you on Nov 15. You'll sign in on PayPal's own page.

Voice

Plain, specific, and exact about money

Write like a careful bookkeeper explaining your statement to you. Use amounts, dates and the names of things; don't cheer and don't apologize.

What to avoid

Things that make it look machine-made

  • Sparkle icons for AIThe agent's actions get the same icons as anything else.
  • Faces for the agentNo robots, brains or avatars. A letter monogram: CL.
  • Gradients, glow and glassFlat sheets on paper. Hatching is the only texture, and it means held money.
  • 3D coins and cardsMoney is shown as numbers and bars drawn to scale.
  • Emoji in the interfaceWords and the five status pills carry the meaning.
  • "AI-powered", "seamless", "smart"Describe what happened instead.
  • Confetti or celebration on spendSpending is never a reward moment.
  • KPI tiles with trend arrowsOne balance, one bar. The ledger does the rest.
  • Every block in a rounded, shadowed cardCards only for things that are separate objects.
