PayPal AI Hackathon · Remit

Your agent spends within its remit.

AI agents can already pay. Remit makes sure they pay for what you agreed to. The agent asks for a budget, you approve it on PayPal, and every purchase has to fit the reason you said yes to.

Cards limit how much an agent can spend. Remit also limits what the money is for.

The problem

Giving an agent your card is all or nothing. A spending limit answers how much, but not for what. A $100 limit lets an agent spend $100 on anything, including something a product listing talked it into. And when a charge looks wrong, all you have is a transaction line, not the reason.

How it works

  1. The agent proposes a budget.One line per need, each with evidence: what past weeks cost, when something was last bought, today's prices.
  2. You approve it on PayPal.That places an authorization hold on your own PayPal account. No new card, no new bank. You can trim or remove lines first.
  3. The agent spends line by line.Each purchase is a partial capture from the hold. It can use its own line and a shared buffer, never another line. An independent purpose check, a second Claude call that never sees the pages the agent read, compares the purchase with the line's reason before any money moves.
  4. Over the limit? It asks.The wallet refuses with a reason, and the agent requests more money with its own reason. Extra money is a new approval and a new hold.
  5. The leftover comes back.At the end of the period, untouched holds are voided right away. The rest is released when the hold expires.

What's new

Agent payment rails, cards with spending limits, and protocols that record what a person authorized already exist. Remit adds the part they leave out: money tied to a stated purpose, and a check that each purchase serves it.

Why PayPal

Authorization holds and partial captures map exactly onto a budget: approving reserves the money, each purchase captures part of it, and what's left is released. The owner uses the PayPal account they already have, and the agent never sees a card number.

What's built

  • Wallet with per-line limits, a shared buffer, one purchase at a time, and reauthorization after PayPal's 3-day honor period
  • PayPal Orders v2 and Payments v2: authorize, partial capture, reauthorize, void, refund
  • Claude Opus 5.5 shopping agent, plus an independent purpose check on every purchase
  • MCP server, so any agent can use the wallet: get_balance, request_budget, request_topup, spend
  • Channel3 product search, and an owner web app with a ledger of reasons

What's next

  • A separate allowance for each connected agent, with its own pause button
  • Approve from your phone instead of the web app
  • Move money between lines with one tap, with no new PayPal approval
  • Accept AP2 mandates, so Remit enforces what they record
  • Pay real merchants that accept PayPal

Tagline

Remit gives AI agents a PayPal budget they have to justify: the agent proposes each line with a reason, you approve a hold, and every purchase is checked against what the money is for.
